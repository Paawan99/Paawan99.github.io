# Remittance Cost Basics

tags: remittance, money transfer, exchange rate, fees

## Two cost components

The real cost of sending money internationally has two parts: an upfront transfer fee (sometimes zero, sometimes a flat amount or a percentage) and an exchange-rate markup, which is the spread a provider adds on top of the real "mid-market" exchange rate (the rate banks and traders use with each other, visible on sites like an FX rate aggregator). Even when a transfer advertises "no fee" or "free," the exchange-rate markup can still make it more expensive overall than a transfer with a small explicit fee but a rate close to mid-market.

## Why banks often hide the real cost

Traditional banks frequently advertise "no transfer fee" international wires while quietly applying a large exchange-rate markup (sometimes several percent below the mid-market rate), so the fee looks attractive but the total amount your recipient gets is lower than it would be with a transparent provider. Because the markup is baked into the exchange rate rather than shown as a separate line item, it's easy to overlook unless you compare the rate you're offered to the live mid-market rate.

## How to estimate true cost

To estimate the real cost of any transfer, compare the amount your recipient will actually receive (after fees and the rate markup) against what they would receive at the true mid-market rate for the same amount sent, then express the difference as a percentage. This "total cost as a percentage of the amount sent" is the only reliable way to compare providers, since fee-only or rate-only comparisons can be misleading.
