# CRS Canadian Work Experience

tags: crs, express entry, canadian experience, noc, teer

## How Canadian experience points work

CRS awards points for skilled work experience gained inside Canada, generally counted in full years (with partial-year experience usually not credited until a full year is reached). Points increase with more years of Canadian experience, up to a cap (commonly five years or more), after which additional years no longer add further core points. Canadian work experience is valuable both for direct CRS points and because it feeds into skill-transferability combination points.

## What counts as eligible experience

To count, work experience generally must be paid, skilled, and full-time (or an equal amount of part-time work), performed while you had authorization to work in Canada, and classified under an eligible occupation in Canada's National Occupational Classification (NOC) system at the TEER (Training, Education, Experience, and Responsibilities) 0, 1, 2, or 3 categories — roughly management, professional, and technical/skilled trades occupations. Experience in TEER 4 or 5 occupations (more routine or elementary work) typically does not qualify.

## Why NOC/TEER eligibility matters

Because CRS Canadian-experience points and Canadian Experience Class (CEC) eligibility both depend on TEER classification, it's important to confirm the correct NOC code for your job duties (not just your job title) before relying on that experience in your profile. Misclassifying a role can affect both your CRS score and your eligibility for an Express Entry program.
