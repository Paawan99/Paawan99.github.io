# CRS Skill Transferability Factors

tags: crs, express entry, skill transferability, education, language, work experience

## Combinations, not standalone scores

Skill transferability points reward candidates whose factors reinforce each other, on the theory that strong language ability plus solid education or Canadian experience predicts better labour-market success. Unlike the core factors, these points come from combining two factors at a time, and the whole category is capped at 100 points even though several sub-combinations exist.

## Education x language

If you have a post-secondary credential and strong official language results (generally CLB 7 or CLB 9 thresholds), you earn points for the combination — more points as both the credential level and the CLB level rise. A candidate with only moderate language scores and a strong degree, or vice versa, earns fewer combination points than one who is strong in both.

## Education x Canadian experience

Similarly, having a post-secondary credential together with Canadian work experience (one year, or two or more years) earns combination points. This rewards candidates who have both formal education and proven Canadian workplace experience, rather than just one or the other.

## Foreign experience x language, and foreign experience x Canadian experience

Foreign (outside Canada) skilled work experience combined with strong language results, or foreign experience combined with Canadian work experience, can also generate combination points, recognizing that international experience plus either language strength or Canadian workplace exposure is a positive signal.

## Certificate of qualification combination

Candidates in a trade occupation who hold a valid certificate of qualification from a Canadian provincial or territorial body, combined with strong official language results, can earn additional transferability points — recognizing skilled trades that may not require a post-secondary degree.
