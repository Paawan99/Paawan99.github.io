CFA Level II & III Risk Concepts

1. Value-at-Risk (VaR) Framework

VaR measures maximum loss at a given confidence level over a time horizon. ENB's 95% daily VaR of -$84.26 per $10K means 95% of days, losses won't exceed this amount. Limitations: ignores tail risk beyond confidence level, assumes historical distributions continue, doesn't capture fat tails. Conditional VaR (Expected Shortfall) of -$156.80 provides more conservative tail risk estimate, addressing VaR limitations.

2. Monte Carlo Simulation in Risk Assessment

Generates thousands of potential future price paths using stochastic processes (Geometric Brownian Motion). Advantages: captures complex correlations, non-linear relationships, fat tails. 10,000 simulations produce distribution of outcomes showing 50% probability of exceeding $75.45 target. More sophisticated than static scenarios but computationally intensive and sensitive to assumption accuracy.

3. Stress Testing & Scenario Analysis

Tests portfolio against extreme but plausible scenarios (recession, rate shock, credit event). Unlike VaR based on historical distributions, stress tests examine tail events beyond historical experience. ENB's -28.4% impact from combined recession/rate shock (1-2% probability) illustrates systemic risk concentration. Useful for risk governance and capital planning.

4. Factor Sensitivity & Risk Decomposition

Decomposes risk into drivers: interest rate sensitivity (12% per 100 bps), EBITDA sensitivity (8.2% per 10%), dividend growth assumptions (0.5% per 1% growth change). Understanding risk drivers enables targeted hedging and scenario planning. Sensitivity analysis identifies which factors warrant monitoring and active management.

5. Systemic vs Idiosyncratic Risk

Beta of 0.78 measures systematic (market) risk - unavoidable for equity holders. Idiosyncratic risk (company-specific) includes regulatory/operational risks. Diversification eliminates idiosyncratic risk; systematic risk requires hedging or acceptance. ENB's low beta suggests limited systematic risk concentration, while energy transition risk is systematic to energy sector broadly.

Risk Management Recommendations & Mitigation Strategies

Comprehensive Risk Management Framework:

1. Interest Rate Risk Mitigation:
  • Lock in current rates on long-term debt (extend maturity profile)
  • Use interest rate swaps to hedge short-term refinancing risk
  • Maintain debt ladder with staggered maturities to reduce roll-over risk
  • Monitor Fed/BoC policy for rate cycle inflection points

2. Credit Risk Management:
  • Target debt/EBITDA ceiling of 2.75x; currently at 2.35x provides buffer
  • Maintain investment-grade ratings (BBB+ or better) through disciplined capital allocation
  • Diversify debt sources; avoid excessive reliance on single financing market
  • Regular covenant monitoring and lender communication

3. Operational Risk Mitigation:
  • Invest in pipeline safety systems (leak detection, preventive maintenance)
  • Cybersecurity investments to protect operational technology systems
  • Project management discipline on capital projects (Line 6B, Line 5)
  • Insurance coverage for catastrophic events

4. Regulatory Risk Mitigation:
  • Proactive stakeholder engagement in regulatory processes
  • Environmental stewardship investments to build social license
  • Diversified geographic footprint reduces single-jurisdiction risk
  • Government relations team to monitor regulatory trends

5. Energy Transition Risk Mitigation:
  • Pivot to renewable energy and hydrogen infrastructure (long-term optionality)
  • Maintain dividend through transition period; signal commitment to shareholders
  • Diversify revenue beyond fossil fuels (carbon capture, hydrogen production)
  • Monitor demand destruction scenarios; stress test terminal value assumptions
