# CRS Core Factors: Age and Education

tags: crs, express entry, age, education, eca

## Age points peak at 20-29

Age points are awarded on a sliding scale and depend on whether you have an accompanying spouse or common-law partner. Without a spouse, candidates aged roughly 20 to 29 receive the maximum age points, and points gradually decrease for candidates in their 30s, 40s, and beyond, reaching zero around age 45. With an accompanying spouse, the maximum age points are lower (because points are also split with the spouse's factors), but the same general shape applies: youth is rewarded, and older applicants receive fewer or no age points.

## More education generally means more points

Education points increase with the level of credential: a high school diploma earns the fewest points, while a master's degree, a professional degree needed to practice in a licensed profession, or a doctoral (PhD) degree earns close to the maximum. Two or more post-secondary credentials, with one being at least three years long, can also score well. As with age, the maximum available education points is higher for candidates without a spouse than for the primary applicant when a spouse is also being scored.

## Foreign credentials need an ECA

If your degree, diploma, or certificate was earned outside Canada, it generally must be assessed through an Educational Credential Assessment (ECA) from an organization designated by IRCC before it can count toward your CRS education points. The ECA confirms that your foreign credential is valid and equivalent to a completed credential in Canada. ECAs typically take weeks to a few months to process and have a validity period, so timing matters if you are close to submitting an Express Entry profile.
