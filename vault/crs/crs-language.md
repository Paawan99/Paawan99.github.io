# CRS Language Points: First and Second Official Language

tags: crs, express entry, language, clb, celpip, ielts, tef, tcf

## First official language points

Every Express Entry candidate must take an approved language test in English (CELPIP-General or IELTS General Training) or French (TEF Canada or TCF Canada) for their first official language, and IRCC converts the test scores into Canadian Language Benchmark (CLB) levels for each of four abilities: speaking, listening, reading, and writing. CRS points are awarded per ability based on the CLB level achieved, so a stronger result in even one ability adds points, and results are typically valid for two years from the test date.

## Second official language points

You can also claim points for a second official language (French if your first language is English, or English if your first language is French) if you have valid test results, though the points available for a second language are smaller than for the first. Adding a credible second-language result, especially French, can meaningfully raise your score even at a modest CLB level.

## Why CLB 9+ matters

CLB 7 is often treated as a baseline threshold for various immigration programs, but CLB 9 and above is a common target for maximizing CRS points, since language points scale up noticeably as you cross benchmark thresholds (particularly around CLB 7, CLB 9, and CLB 10). Reaching CLB 9 in all four abilities also unlocks larger skill-transferability combination points (see the skill transferability note) and can be the single biggest lever for raising a borderline CRS score.
