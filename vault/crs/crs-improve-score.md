# Practical Ways to Improve Your CRS Score

tags: crs, express entry, category-based draws, french, pnp

## Retake your language test

Because language points scale steeply around CLB thresholds (especially CLB 7, 9, and 10) and also feed into skill-transferability combination points, improving even one ability's score on a retake can raise your CRS meaningfully more than almost any other single action. Many candidates find retaking CELPIP, IELTS, TEF, or TCF the fastest lever available to them.

## Add French

Gaining French proficiency (even alongside strong English) can unlock the French-language bonus (+25 or +50 points) and opens eligibility for French-language category-based draws, which have had some of the lowest cut-off scores in recent Express Entry rounds. This makes French a high-leverage option for candidates who can invest time in language study.

## Gain Canadian work experience

Time spent working in Canada in an eligible NOC TEER 0/1/2/3 occupation adds direct core points and boosts skill-transferability combinations with education and language. Even a change from zero to one year of Canadian experience can produce a noticeable score jump.

## Get an ECA done early

If you have foreign education, completing an Educational Credential Assessment (ECA) is required before that education counts toward CRS points. Because ECAs take time to process and expire after a set period, getting one done early (and renewing before it lapses) avoids losing points unexpectedly right before submitting a profile.

## Pursue a Provincial Nominee Program

A PNP nomination adds 600 points and effectively guarantees an ITA, making it the single largest lever available, though it usually comes with a commitment to the nominating province. See the dedicated PNP note for details.

## Watch category-based draws

IRCC periodically runs category-based Express Entry draws targeting specific groups — recent categories have included French-language proficiency, healthcare occupations, STEM occupations, skilled trades, and agriculture/agri-food. These draws can have lower cut-off scores than general draws, so candidates who qualify for a targeted category should confirm their profile reflects the relevant occupation or language proficiency, and watch IRCC's announcements for which categories are active each round.
