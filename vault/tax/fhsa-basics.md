# FHSA Basics (First Home Savings Account)

tags: tax, benefits, newcomer

## What it is

The First Home Savings Account (FHSA) is a registered account designed to help eligible first-time home buyers save for a down payment. It combines features of both an RRSP and a TFSA: contributions can reduce your taxable income, and qualifying withdrawals toward a first home are completely tax-free.

## Deductible in, tax-free out

Like an RRSP, contributions to an FHSA are tax-deductible, lowering your taxable income for the year. Like a TFSA, growth inside the account is not taxed, and if you use the funds for a qualifying first home purchase, the withdrawal is also tax-free. This "deductible in, tax-free out" combination is unique among Canada's registered accounts and generally makes the FHSA a priority account for eligible first-time buyers.

## Annual and lifetime caps

The FHSA has both an annual contribution limit and an overall lifetime contribution limit. Unused annual room can typically be carried forward to a later year (up to a cap), but only after you've opened the account — unlike the TFSA, room does not accumulate before you open one. Because these limits are set by legislation and could be adjusted, these figures change yearly — verify the current annual and lifetime limits on canada.ca.

## Who qualifies as a first-time buyer

To open an FHSA, you generally need to be a Canadian resident, at least 18 (or the age of majority in your province), and a "first-time home buyer" — meaning you (and generally your spouse/common-law partner) have not owned a home that you lived in as your principal place of residence during the current calendar year or the preceding four calendar years. This means someone who owned a home longer ago may still qualify again after that window. Account holders must also close their FHSA(s) within a set number of years of first opening one, or by a certain age, whichever comes first — check current rules on canada.ca.
