DuPont Analysis: ROE Decomposition

What is DuPont Analysis?

DuPont Analysis decomposes ROE into three components: Net Profit Margin (profitability), Asset Turnover (efficiency), and Equity Multiplier (leverage). This framework reveals which factors drive shareholder returns and identifies improvement opportunities.

2025 DuPont Decomposition:
  • Net Profit Margin: 6.8%
  • Asset Turnover: 0.22x
  • Equity Multiplier: 2.89x
  • ROE = 6.8% × 0.22 × 2.89 = 4.3% (operational ROE)

Enbridge's ROE improvement is driven by margin expansion and controlled leverage. The low asset turnover is typical for capital-intensive infrastructure businesses. The 1.54 D/E ratio reflects optimal capital structure for utility regulation.

5-Year Historical Financial Performance

Historical Trends: Enbridge demonstrates steady revenue growth averaging 2.1% annually over the 5-year period (2021-2025), reflecting the stable nature of regulated pipeline infrastructure. EBITDA growth of 2.8% outpaces revenue growth, indicating operating leverage and margin expansion from cost management initiatives. EPS has grown from C$2.78 to C$3.23 (16.2% total), demonstrating earnings accretion through both organic growth and disciplined capital allocation. Operating margins have improved from 38.1% to 42.1%, driven by operational efficiencies and favorable regulatory outcomes.

CFA Level II & III Concepts Applied

1. DuPont Analysis (Fundamental Analysis)

Decomposing ROE into net margin, asset turnover, and leverage reveals that Enbridge's shareholder returns are driven primarily by margin expansion (6.8%) supported by modest leverage (2.89x multiplier). This indicates strong operational performance without excessive financial risk.

2. Free Cash Flow to Equity (FCFE) Analysis

FCFE of $10.4B annually (2025) represents cash available to equity holders after debt service. With 2.2B shares outstanding, FCFE per share of $4.73 supports the current dividend of $3.88 (54% payout ratio) with substantial room for growth and special distributions.

3. WACC as Discount Rate

The 6.54% WACC reflects the blended cost of capital considering 1.54x D/E ratio and 8.2% cost of equity. Using WACC as the discount rate in valuation ensures consistency with both operating returns and capital structure assumptions, incorporating default risk premiums implicit in debt costs.

4. Terminal Value Sensitivity

Terminal value represents ~65% of total DCF value, creating significant sensitivity to long-term growth assumptions. A 0.1% change in perpetual growth rate changes valuation by ~3%, highlighting the importance of reasonable terminal growth assumptions consistent with long-term GDP growth.

Investment Summary & Recommendation

Investment Thesis: Enbridge represents a stable, dividend-paying infrastructure investment suitable for conservative and income-focused investors. The company's regulated utility model provides earnings visibility, strong cash generation, and a sustainable dividend supported by long-term regulatory certainty. With 27 consecutive years of dividend increases and a 54% payout ratio, the company balances income generation with financial flexibility.

Strengths: Market-leading position in North American energy infrastructure; stable, regulated cash flows; low commodity price exposure; strong dividend growth history; solid balance sheet with manageable leverage; improving operational efficiency.

Headwinds: Energy transition risk from declining fossil fuel demand; regulatory pressure on pipeline expansion; interest rate sensitivity for utility stocks; execution risk on major capital projects; potential dividend growth moderation if earnings growth slows.

Valuation: Trading at fair value relative to peers and historical multiples. The current price reflects quality premium appropriate for defensive infrastructure with inflation-protected, regulated returns. Suitable as core holding in dividend portfolio rather than growth investment.
