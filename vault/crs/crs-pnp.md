# Provincial Nominee Programs (PNP) and CRS

tags: crs, express entry, pnp, provincial nominee

## How a nomination adds 600 points

Each Canadian province and territory (except Quebec, which runs its own separate immigration system) operates Provincial Nominee Program streams that let them nominate candidates for permanent residence based on local labour-market or economic needs. When a province nominates an Express Entry candidate, IRCC adds 600 points to that candidate's CRS score, which in practice puts them at or near the maximum score and virtually guarantees an Invitation to Apply (ITA) at the next relevant draw.

## Express Entry-aligned streams

Not all PNP streams work through Express Entry — some are "base" PNP streams with their own separate application process and no direct CRS boost. The streams that matter for CRS are the "enhanced" or Express Entry-aligned PNP streams, where the province nominates a candidate who already has an Express Entry profile, triggering the 600-point boost. Each province sets its own eligibility criteria (occupation lists, connection to the province, work experience, education, or a job offer in that province), and criteria change often, so check the specific province's immigration website for current streams and requirements.

## Trade-offs to weigh

A provincial nomination usually comes with a condition or expectation to reside and work in the nominating province, at least initially. Some candidates target a PNP specifically to reach ITA-guaranteed territory quickly, while others prefer to wait for a strong core CRS score and more geographic flexibility. Processing times, application windows, and quotas vary by province and can change year to year.
