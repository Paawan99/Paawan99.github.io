# Filing Your First Tax Return in Canada

tags: tax, benefits, newcomer

## Becoming a tax resident

You generally become a Canadian tax resident once you establish significant residential ties here — things like a home, a spouse or common-law partner, or dependants in Canada. Many newcomers become tax residents on the date they land (arrive with permanent resident status) or, for some temporary residents, on the date they establish those ties. Your "residency start date" matters because it affects which credits and benefits you can claim for your first year. If you're unsure of your exact residency start date, the CRA has guidance and you may want to speak with a tax professional.

## The filing deadline

For most individuals, the tax filing deadline is April 30 of the following year (for example, your 2025 tax year return is due by April 30, 2026). If you or your spouse/common-law partner are self-employed, the filing deadline is extended, but any balance owing is still generally due April 30. These figures and dates change yearly — verify on canada.ca before you file.

## Why file even with no income or low income

Many newcomers assume that if they had little or no income, they don't need to file. In Canada, filing a tax return is actually how you apply for many income-tested benefits and credits — the CRA doesn't automatically know your income unless you tell it. Filing (even a "nil" return) is typically required to start receiving the GST/HST credit, the Canada Carbon Rebate, the Canada Child Benefit, and provincial equivalents. Filing early in your first year, even with $0 income, can unlock ongoing quarterly payments.

## What you'll need to file

Common documents and information include: your Social Insurance Number (SIN); T4 slips from employers (summarizing employment income and deductions); a T2202 tuition tax certificate if you were enrolled at a designated post-secondary institution; and, in some provinces, rent receipts or a landlord's information to claim provincial rent/property tax credits. If this is your first Canadian return, you may also need to report the date you arrived and, in some cases, your worldwide income for the part of the year before you became a resident.

## Free ways to file

The CRA maintains a list of certified tax software, and many providers offer a free tier for simple returns (some are free regardless of income, others are free below an income threshold). Community volunteer tax clinics (the Community Volunteer Income Tax Program, CVITP) also file simple returns for free for eligible individuals, including many newcomers and students. Always check canada.ca for the current list of certified free software, since offerings change yearly.
