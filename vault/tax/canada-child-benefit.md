# Canada Child Benefit (CCB)

tags: tax, benefits, newcomer

## Tax-free monthly payment

The Canada Child Benefit is a tax-free monthly payment made to eligible families to help with the cost of raising children under 18. Because it's tax-free, you don't report it as income and it doesn't reduce other tax credits the way taxable income would. Many provinces and territories also fold in their own child benefit as part of the same monthly payment.

## Under-6 vs. 6-to-17 amounts

The CCB pays a higher maximum annual amount per child under age 6 than for children aged 6 through 17, reflecting the generally higher cost of care for younger children. The exact maximum annual amounts per child are set for each July-to-June benefit year and adjusted for inflation, so these figures change yearly — verify current maximums on canada.ca.

## Income phase-out

The amount you receive is based on your (and your spouse/common-law partner's) adjusted family net income, and the benefit is gradually reduced as income rises above a set threshold, with a lower reduction rate for the first threshold and a higher rate above a second, higher threshold. Families with more children see the benefit reduced at a slightly different pace due to how the phase-out formula is structured. Because thresholds and rates are updated annually, verify current numbers on canada.ca.

## How to apply

Many families are automatically considered for the CCB when they register a newborn's birth with their province or territory, if they consent to share information with the CRA during registration. Otherwise, you can apply directly using Form RC66 (Canada Child Benefits Application), which you may need if you're a newcomer, have recently gained custody of a child, or didn't apply at birth registration. You generally also need to be filing (or have filed) a tax return, since eligibility is reassessed every year based on your return.

## Estimates only

Because amounts depend on income, number and ages of children, and the province or territory, only the CRA's official CCB calculator or your CRA My Account can give you an accurate, current estimate. Treat any dollar figures you see elsewhere as rough estimates that change yearly — verify on canada.ca.
