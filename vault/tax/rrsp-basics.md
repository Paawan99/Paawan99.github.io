# RRSP Basics (Registered Retirement Savings Plan)

tags: tax, benefits, newcomer

## Deductible contributions

Contributions to a Registered Retirement Savings Plan (RRSP) are tax-deductible, meaning they reduce your taxable income for the year you claim the deduction (you can contribute in one year and choose to claim the deduction in a later year if that's more advantageous). Investment growth inside the RRSP is not taxed while it stays in the account, letting your savings compound faster than in a regular taxable account.

## How contribution room is calculated

Your RRSP contribution room ("deduction limit") is generally 18% of your previous year's earned income, up to an annual dollar maximum set by the government, plus any unused room carried forward from prior years, minus any pension adjustment if you belong to an employer pension plan. The annual dollar maximum is indexed and changes yearly — verify the current year's limit on canada.ca or in your CRA My Account, which shows your personal deduction limit.

## Taxed on withdrawal

Unlike a TFSA, money you withdraw from an RRSP is generally added to your taxable income in the year you withdraw it, and is taxed at your marginal rate at that time. The idea is that you get a tax break now (during your working years, often at a higher tax bracket) and pay tax later in retirement, potentially at a lower bracket. Withdrawals are also subject to withholding tax deducted at source by your financial institution.

## Home Buyers' Plan (HBP) and Lifelong Learning Plan (LLP)

Two special programs let you withdraw from your RRSP without immediate tax, provided you repay the amount over time: the Home Buyers' Plan (HBP) allows eligible first-time home buyers to withdraw up to a set limit toward a home purchase, and the Lifelong Learning Plan (LLP) allows withdrawals to fund full-time education or training for you or your spouse/partner. Both require repayment installments over a number of years, and unrepaid amounts are added to your taxable income. Limits and repayment periods change from time to time, so check canada.ca for current rules before using either program.
