# TFSA Basics (Tax-Free Savings Account)

tags: tax, benefits, newcomer

## Tax-free growth

A Tax-Free Savings Account (TFSA) lets any growth — interest, dividends, or capital gains — accumulate and be withdrawn completely tax-free. Unlike an RRSP, contributions are not tax-deductible, but you also never pay tax on qualifying withdrawals, which makes the TFSA a flexible, general-purpose savings and investing vehicle available to eligible Canadian residents.

## Contribution room: age 18 and Canadian residency

TFSA contribution room begins accumulating in the year you turn 18 and hold a valid Social Insurance Number, but only for years in which you are also a resident of Canada for tax purposes. If you were 18 or older but not yet a Canadian resident in a given year, you generally do not accumulate TFSA room for that year. This is a key point for newcomers: your TFSA room typically starts from the year you become a tax resident (or turn 18, if later), not from the year Canada introduced the TFSA. Room can only be used once you actually open a TFSA account, which generally requires being a resident with a valid SIN.

## Withdraw anytime

You can withdraw from a TFSA at any time, for any reason, without owing tax on the withdrawal. Withdrawn amounts are added back to your available contribution room, but only starting the following calendar year — not immediately.

## Over-contribution penalty

If you contribute more than your available TFSA room, the CRA charges a penalty tax of 1% per month on the excess amount, for each month it remains in the account. This can add up quickly, so it's important to track your available room (shown in your CRA My Account) before contributing, especially if you have multiple TFSA accounts at different institutions. These penalty rates and rules can be updated, so verify current details on canada.ca.

## Newcomer note

If you immigrated to Canada partway through your life, your cumulative TFSA room is based only on the years since you became a resident (and were 18+), not on every year since the TFSA program began. Many newcomers mistakenly assume they have the same room as someone who was a resident the whole time — double-check your CRA-reported room before making a large contribution.
