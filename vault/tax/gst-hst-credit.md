# GST/HST Credit

tags: tax, benefits, newcomer

## What it is

The GST/HST credit is a tax-free quarterly payment from the federal government that helps individuals and families with low and modest incomes offset some of the GST or HST they pay. It's automatically considered alongside any related provincial or territorial credit that's paid together with it, depending on where you live.

## Quarterly payments

Payments are typically issued four times a year — historically around July, October, January, and April. The amount you receive is recalculated each benefit year (July to June) based on your prior year's tax return. These payment dates and the benefit-year structure can shift slightly, so verify the current schedule on canada.ca.

## Income-tested

The credit amount depends on your (and your spouse/common-law partner's, if applicable) net family income and the number of children registered for the Canada Child Benefit. As income rises above a certain threshold, the credit is gradually reduced ("phased out") until it reaches zero. Because thresholds and phase-out rates are adjusted for inflation, these figures change yearly — verify on canada.ca.

## How you get it

For most people, there's no separate application: filing your annual tax return is what triggers the CRA to assess your eligibility and start payments automatically. Newcomers to Canada who don't yet have a filed tax return on record often need to apply directly using Form RC151 (GST/HST Credit Application for Individuals Who Become Residents of Canada) for the year they arrive, since the CRA has no prior return to assess. In later years, filing your regular return is enough.

## Rough amounts (estimates only)

Annual GST/HST credit amounts are commonly discussed in the range of a few hundred dollars for a single adult, with additional amounts for a spouse/partner and per child, paid out over the four quarterly instalments. These are rough, illustrative figures only — actual amounts depend on income, family size, and the benefit year, and they change yearly. Always verify current amounts on canada.ca or in your CRA My Account.
