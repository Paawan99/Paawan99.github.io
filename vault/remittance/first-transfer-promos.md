# First-Transfer Promotions: Reading the Fine Print

tags: remittance, promotions, first transfer, fine print

## How first-transfer promos work

Many money transfer providers advertise an especially favorable exchange rate or a fee waiver for your first transfer, or first transfer above a minimum amount, as a customer-acquisition incentive. The promotional rate is often noticeably closer to the mid-market rate than the provider's standard ongoing rate, which is why it's tempting to judge a provider's overall value based on that first experience alone.

## Why the fine print matters

Promotional offers typically come with conditions: a maximum promotional amount (extra funds above that cap may transfer at the standard rate), a limited time window to use the offer, restrictions to specific corridors or delivery methods, and sometimes a requirement to sign up through a specific referral link or app version. After the first qualifying transfer, the rate usually reverts to the provider's standard markup, which can be meaningfully worse than the promotional rate you experienced initially.

## How to evaluate a provider despite the promo

Because the first transfer isn't representative of ongoing cost, simulate a second, non-promotional transfer using the provider's calculator (or check their published standard exchange-rate margin) before deciding to make that provider your regular choice. Compare that standard, ongoing rate against competitors' standard rates, not against their own promos, to get a fair sense of long-term cost — then decide separately whether it's worth using different providers' promos opportunistically for one-off large transfers.
