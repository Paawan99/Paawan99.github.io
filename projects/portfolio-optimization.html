Modern Portfolio Theory Approach: Portfolio optimization applies Markowitz's mean-variance framework to construct an efficient frontier of portfolios. By identifying the optimal combination of assets, we maximize risk-adjusted returns (Sharpe ratio). The framework incorporates expected returns, volatility, and correlation matrices to identify holdings that reduce portfolio risk while maintaining desired return targets.

CFA Level III Portfolio Concepts

1. Markowitz Mean-Variance Framework

The foundational optimization approach maximizes portfolio return for a given level of risk. The efficient frontier represents portfolios where no other portfolio offers higher return at the same risk level. Our optimization solves: Maximize E(Rp) subject to σp = Target Risk, where E(Rp) = ∑w_i*E(R_i) and σp = √(∑∑w_i*w_j*Cov_ij).

2. Sharpe Ratio & Risk-Adjusted Returns

The Sharpe Ratio (0.489) measures excess return per unit of total risk: SR = (Rp - Rf) / σp. Our portfolio's 0.489 indicates superior risk-adjusted performance versus the market's 0.373. A higher Sharpe ratio means more efficient use of risk. This metric is crucial for comparing portfolios and identifying value-added from manager skill versus luck.

3. Jensen's Alpha & Value-Added Assessment

Jensen's Alpha (+0.89%) measures performance beyond CAPM prediction: α = Rp - [Rf + β(Rm - Rf)]. Our positive alpha indicates the portfolio generates excess returns after accounting for systematic risk taken. This suggests value-added from strategic allocation and security selection, not from taking additional market risk (beta of only 0.83).

4. Diversification Benefit & Correlation Management

Portfolio standard deviation (11.8%) is significantly lower than the equal-weighted average of individual volatilities (14.2%), demonstrating the power of diversification. This benefit comes from combining assets with average correlation of 0.62 to the market. The formula σp = √(∑w_i²σ_i² + 2∑∑w_i*w_j*ρ_ij*σ_i*σ_j) shows how lower correlations reduce portfolio risk.

5. Capital Allocation Line & Optimal Portfolio Selection

The CAL connects the risk-free rate (3.5%) to the optimal risk portfolio, showing the best achievable risk-return tradeoff. All efficient portfolios lie on this line. Investor-specific risk tolerance determines position on the CAL (conservative investors closer to Rf, aggressive investors at optimal portfolio or beyond via leverage).

Portfolio Rebalancing Strategy

Rebalancing Framework: To maintain optimal allocations, quarterly rebalancing is recommended when any position drifts >1% from target allocation. This disciplined approach forces "buy low, sell high" behavior. For Enbridge specifically, maintain 8-9% allocation target. If price appreciation increases ENB to 9.5% of portfolio (due to 3% price gain), trim 0.5% and redeploy to underweight sectors. Rebalancing also provides tax-loss harvesting opportunities in declining positions and crystalizes gains in appreciated holdings.

Annual Review: Conduct comprehensive portfolio review annually to reassess expected returns, volatility, and correlations based on new market data. Adjust allocations if fundamental assumptions change (interest rates, dividend policies, sector outlooks). ENB allocation may be reduced if dividend growth slows below 2% or increased if yield compression appears terminal.

Portfolio Implementation & Conclusion

Implementation Strategy: The optimal portfolio allocation should be implemented systematically over 6-8 weeks to minimize market-timing risk and execution costs. Begin by establishing core positions in lower-liquidity holdings (REITs: 8%, Small-caps: 2%), then build positions in highly liquid holdings (Financials, ENB, Technology). Use limit orders to avoid market-impact costs on larger positions.
