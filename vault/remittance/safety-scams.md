# Sending Money Safely and Avoiding Scams

tags: remittance, scams, fintrac, fraud, safety

## Use regulated providers

In Canada, legitimate money services businesses (MSBs) that transmit funds must register with FINTRAC (the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada), Canada's financial intelligence and anti-money-laundering regulator. Before trusting a new provider, verify it appears on FINTRAC's public registry of money services businesses, and be cautious of apps or individuals offering transfers outside any regulated channel, even if they promise better rates.

## Common remittance scams

Watch for: unsolicited requests to send money urgently to a "relative in trouble," fake customer-support numbers found via search ads rather than the provider's official site, phishing messages claiming a transfer is "on hold" and asking for banking credentials or one-time codes, and informal "hawala"-style or social-media-arranged transfers that bypass any regulator and offer no recourse if something goes wrong. Legitimate providers will never ask you to share a one-time password or full banking password over phone or chat.

## Practical safety steps

Only use providers you can verify are regulated, always initiate transfers from the official app or website (not a link in an email or text), double-check the recipient's name and account/mobile details before confirming, keep transfer confirmation numbers and receipts, and treat any "send money to unlock a bigger payment" request as a scam. If you suspect fraud, contact your provider immediately and report it to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre.
